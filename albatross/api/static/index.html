      How the books work

      Paper trades only: no real money is behind any of these. Each book follows one rule from its first day, and every number is after fees, slippage, price impact and gas. The memecoin rules were fixed on Oct 2 without looking at the result of any call that had a dossier score. Factor rank was picked on its history, so only its live weeks count. The books are rebuilt from the call ledger every 15 minutes.

      A trade counts once it is settled, the same way in every book of a kind: after 24 hours for the memecoin books, 6 hours for the opposite of the quant model, and at the stop, target or horizon for the Strategist's ideas. Until then it shows as open. A book gets a verdict only once it has enough trades, and each card says when that is due.       What the lab has found

      How the lab works

      Paper only: no real money is behind any of this. Every hour the lab makes about ten new strategies, variations and crosses of the best so far plus random ones, and once a day the strategy researcher proposes about thirty more. No strategy is tested twice.

      A strategy has to pass two tests. On the older data its average result has to be positive with a t-statistic above the bar. The bar rises as more strategies are tried, because the more you try, the more look good by luck. On the recent data, which nothing is fitted on, it has to stay positive and do at least as well as the baseline.

      Survivors trade on paper, up to 12 memecoin and 8 factor strategies at once. A memecoin strategy is ruled on after two weeks and 100 trades, a factor strategy after six weeks. It becomes a champion only if it stays positive with a t of at least 2.45 and beats the baseline over the same days. One that is clearly losing is retired early. Champions are evidence for the owner to weigh: nothing trades on them.

      Memecoin numbers are the net % of a $100 trade after every cost, one position per coin, the same trades the paper books count. Factor numbers are the net % of each holding period after a 0.1% cost each way, funding left out.

      How to test it

      Every memecoin call gets a verdict the moment it lands, from the coin's dossier as it stood then: SKIP (with the reason), PASS, or NO DOSSIER when none existed yet. It is the rebuilt checklist: skip coins under 24 hours old, a Dex listing still unpaid after a day, and the dev wallet, liquidity, mint, tax, top-10 and organic red flags. A call's result is a $100 buy at the call's price after every cost, held 24 hours.

      The test is every call from Oct 8, 22:00 UTC on: the checklist was written from calls that had finished before then. The feed below opens on finished calls, so each row already has its answer; switch to "Still open" to pick some to check tomorrow. Each call counts once at its own price, so a coin called five times counts five times, and a few big winners lift the average, so read the median beside it. Paper only: the bot's buys are read from records we already keep, and nothing is sent to the bot.

      How these are measured

      Paper only. Every number is a $100 group call after every cost, one position per coin, the same trades the paper books count, from Sep 30. "Against every call" compares each rule with every group call day by day, because the whole memecoin market's mood swings from one day to the next: it shows the average difference in points a trade and on how many days the rule was ahead.

      Factor rank, week by week

      How these are measured

      Factor rank orders the liquid Hyperliquid coins every day. Once a week, on a fixed Monday, we check whether that day's order matched the next 7 days' returns: the rank correlation is 1 for a perfect match, 0 for no order and below 0 for the wrong order. The gap is the top fifth's return minus the bottom fifth's, before costs and funding. The weeks before Sep 29 are the ranking's history, computed the same way after the fact; from then on it ran live.

        Reading the columns

        The first column reads raw data: candles, funding, headlines, X posts, the web. One step removed means an agent reads at least one model's conclusions; two steps removed, conclusions built on conclusions.

        No loops

        No agent's conclusion comes back to it as someone else's evidence; a test fails the build if one ever does. Dotted wires are attention only: they decide what an agent looks at, never what it concludes.

        How grades are measured

        The weekly review grades direction calls against a coin flip with the same mix of longs and shorts, and ideas and trades in R after trading costs, against breaking even. Sources also shows each source by timeframe against a one-way caller (always long or always short, whichever did better). Pause or rescale any agent on the Output page.
